1. What happens when the number of hidden nodes increase?
With very few hidden units (1-2), the model is too simple to represent a
non-linear decision boundary, so it can only draw something close to a
straight line and badly underfits the flower-shaped dataset (~67% accuracy).
Once the hidden layer has 3 or more units, the model gains enough capacity
to bend the decision boundary into the flower's curved petal shape, and
accuracy jumps to ~90%. Beyond that, adding many more units (20, 50) does
not meaningfully change the decision boundary or accuracy any further.

2. Can you explain the pattern of the accuracy when the hidden nodes increase?
The pattern is underfitting -> good fit -> diminishing returns. Below 3
units, the network lacks the capacity to separate the two classes and
underfits. Around 3-5 units, it has just enough capacity to fit the data
well, and accuracy peaks (~91% at 5 units). Past that, extra hidden units
add representational power the 2D dataset does not need, so accuracy
plateaus rather than improving further -- the bottleneck stops being model
capacity and becomes the inherent noise/overlap in the data itself. With a
much larger or noisier dataset, over-parameterizing like this could
eventually cause overfitting instead of a plateau.
